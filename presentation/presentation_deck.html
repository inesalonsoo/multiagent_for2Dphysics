An agentic loop for
stochastic rare-event analysis

AI agents set up the analysis of a hopping particle under a code-based physics check, and every result is compared with the exactly computed answer. Moiré stacking-domain switching is the motivation, not a modeled system.

Inés Alonso · moiré-msm-engine

github.com/inesalonsoo/multiagent_for2Dphysics

Motivation

Can AI agents be trusted to set up a scientific analysis, if every result is checked against an exactly known answer?

We test this on a problem whose true answer we can compute, so nothing rests on another model or a fit.

Where moiré materials fit: motivation, not model Real twisted-bilayer stacking domains have a two-component displacement field with three-fold symmetry and relax as an elastic network, not as one particle in a double well. Nothing here is derived from moiré physics; the tilt b is a toy stand-in for symmetry breaking. Moiré motivates why verified switching rates matter.
The system

A particle in a double well

dx = −V′(x) dt + √(2/β) dW

V(x) = A(x² − 1)² + b·x

Picture a ball in two valleys, shaken by heat. It rattles inside one valley and now and then hops over the hill. β measures how cold the system is: higher β, rarer hops.

The hopping rate can be computed exactly from this equation (a one-second calculation), and so can how the time is shared between the valleys. The textbook Eyring–Kramers formula is only the cold limit: it overestimates the rate by 7–11% at the temperatures used here.

Why start in 0-D In 0-D the answer is exact. For the 2D field the barrier grows with the system's area, and the rate has no known closed form (Rolland, Bouchet & Simonnet, arXiv:1507.05577, §3.2.1).
Phase 1: the physics

The analysis recovers the exact rate

Top: measured rate (6 simulations per β) against the exact rate and the Eyring–Kramers limit. Bottom: deviation from the exact rate of the simulated chain.

How the model is built

A Markov State Model cuts the path into 50 regions and counts the moves between them, looking a fixed time apart (the lag), set to a tenth of the slowest timescale it finds.

Two valleys, really

One slow process in all 42 measurements (t₂/t₃ ≥ 40.8 at the chosen lag; a single-valley control gives below 2), and the model predicts its own longer-lag behavior (Chapman–Kolmogorov error ≤ 0.03).

The rate

Consistent with the exact rate for every β the data resolve (3–9): no difference at the 1% level, with 1–3% precision at β = 3–5. At β = 10 the simulations see too few hops, and we report that instead of a number.

Phase 2: error bars

The built-in error bars are not honest

The MSM software reports Bayesian error bars. We checked them against the exact answer.

2 of 42

90% intervals that contain the exact rate. Honest intervals would contain it about 38 times.

11–193×

How much narrower they are than the real spread between independent simulations.

WhyThe software treats millions of overlapping, strongly correlated observations as if they were nearly independent, so it believes it knows far more than it does.
What we use insteadThe spread between 6 independent simulations, measured directly.
Phase 3: the AI agents

Orchestrator · Optimizer · Validator

Mirrors the Prover / Verifier split of Axiomatic AI's Ax-Prover (arXiv:2510.12787, Breen et al.): the agent that proposes cannot grade itself.

Orchestrator

no AI, no physics

Runs the analysis on each proposal, routes the results, and decides when to stop, by a fixed rule.

Optimizer

≙ Prover

An AI that proposes settings (number of regions, lag time). Asked not to predict its own score: a prompted rule, not an enforced one.

Validator

≙ Verifier

Checks the physics in ordinary code before any AI is asked: one slow process, and a rate within ±5.6% of the exact value (3 × the spread of 6 simulations). Its AI only comments; it cannot overturn a check.

every round → one JSON ledger entry (settings, measurements, verdict, reasoning)

What the re-check showedThe original check compared against Eyring–Kramers, 8% above the simulated chain's exact rate. It accepted only short-lag settings (about 5% high) and rejected accurate ones. Against the exact rate, all 17 settings fall inside the ±5.6% band, so this absolute check cannot catch a 5% bias.
What still holdsRuns explore differently (shared first steps, then divergent paths), and the AI cannot override a verdict. The rate's fall with lag is clear within one simulation, so requiring a converged lag, as Phase 1 does, would catch the bias.
Phase 3.5: formal proofs

Proving the math behind the checks

The physics rests on facts about the landscape: its slope, where the valleys are, how curved they are, how high the hill is. The Lean proof assistant accepts a proof only if every step is correct. We wrote 7 statements in Lean 4 + Mathlib; provers write the proofs and never edit the statements.

theorem curvature_at_saddle (A : ℝ) :

2 proved: the left-right symmetry (by ax-prover) and the slope formula V_hasDerivAt (by Lemma, a separate tool, at my direction). Lean's axiom check shows neither relies on an unproved step; the 5 open statements still do. The curvature and barrier facts back the Eyring–Kramers comparison; the exact rates are computed numerically. This project's main error (treating an approximate rate as exact) was outside what Lean checks. Out of scope by design: the Eyring–Kramers formula itself, MSM convergence, and exact tilted-well positions.

Reflection

What this project taught me

1 · Learned the physics from scratch

  • Kramers escape theory, detailed balance, and why a rate and an equilibrium ratio are different kinds of "exact."
  • Read Rolland, Bouchet & Simonnet (arXiv:1507.05577) in full.

2 · An approximation is not an answer key

  • Eyring–Kramers is exact only for very cold systems; as our reference it was 9% off at β = 5.
  • Our early "agreement" came from two errors cancelling. Computing the exact rate took one second.

3 · Error bars must be tested, not assumed

  • An error budget built from the observed deviation can never fail, so it proves nothing.
  • The Bayesian error bars covered the truth 2 times in 42. Coverage against a known answer is the real test.

4 · MSM lag times converge slowly

  • Waiting until the rate barely changes between lags stops too early.
  • The measured rate approaches the truth from above as the lag grows.

5 · Handing an optimizer the answer is not a demonstration of search

  • An early prompt leaked the lag then believed converged (20 frames, now known to be about 5% biased), and all 8 runs proposed it on their first try.
  • Removing the leak let a real search happen. Its checks, in turn, are only as good as the reference they use.
