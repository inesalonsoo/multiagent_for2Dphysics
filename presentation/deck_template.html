Motivation

Can a multi-agent LLM system be trusted to discover an analysis pipeline when every claim it makes is checked against a known, closed-form answer?

A narrow question, where every result below is checked against an exact physical answer, not against another model or a fit.

Where moiré materials fit: motivation, not derivation Real twisted-bilayer-graphene stacking domains sit on a 2-component displacement field with three-fold (AA/AB/BA) symmetry, and relax as an elastic soliton network, not a scalar double well, not single-particle thermal hopping. Nothing here derives from moiré elasticity; the tilt b is a toy symmetry-breaking stand-in. Moiré motivates why verified switching-rate discovery matters, it is not what Phase 4 models.
The system

The 0-D stochastic double well

dx = −V′(x) dt + √(2/β) dW

V(x) = A(x² − 1)² + b·x

Two minima, one barrier, one tilt parameter. Chosen deliberately: it's one of the few stochastic systems where both sides of the check are closed-form — the Eyring–Kramers rate's exponent exact and its prefactor asymptotically exact (β→∞), matching to ~15-20% at the β this project uses, and the Boltzmann population ratio exact by symmetry. No fitting required on either side.

Why 0-D, not the 2D field directly The Eyring–Kramers prefactor has no known closed form in 2D (Rolland, Bouchet & Simonnet, arXiv:1507.05577, §3.2.1). 0-D is the reference case every extended-field result in that literature is checked against. This project does the same.
Phase 1: the verified engine

The physics is real

Two-panel figure: log-rate (top) vs. the analytical line; % deviation from analytical (bottom, linear). This was added specifically so Phase 2's error budget is visible, not just claimed.

Gate 1: two macrostates

Exactly 2 recovered by PCCA+ at every β, 6/6 replicas. Clean.

Gate 2: Arrhenius slope

Measured −0.981 vs. exact −1 (1.87% deviation, β ≤ 7) — gated on a 10% relative tolerance, not statistical significance: Eyring–Kramers is a β→∞ asymptotic formula, so a real O(1/β) correction exists at any finite β and doesn't shrink with more data.

Two real bugs found en route

Sparse-transition-count bias at high β, and a lag time (fixed at 20 frames) that wasn't actually converged. This was found by scanning the implied-timescale plateau, not assumed.

Phase 3: the agentic loop

Orchestrator · Optimizer · Validator

Mirrors the Prover/Verifier separation of Axiomatic AI's Ax-Prover (arXiv:2510.12787, Breen et al.) — an independent, grounded verifier that the proposer cannot talk its way past.

Orchestrator

no LLM, no physics

Task assignment, feedback routing, owns the stop decision. A pure function of the verdict and iteration count.

Optimizer

≙ Prover

Proposes (n_clusters, lag) via a deterministic tool call. Told not to predict its own score — a prompted norm, not schema-enforced like the other two boxes here.

Validator

≙ Verifier

Computes hard physics checks in Python before the LLM sees them. Verdict is schema-enforced, not the LLM's opinion.

every iteration → one JSON ledger entry (config, result, verdict, reasoning)

Phase 3.5: formal verification

The known answers, formally stated

Every physics check in Phase 3 leans on known_answers.py's hardcoded constants — curvature, critical points, barrier height — derived once by hand in a docstring and never independently checked since. Phase 3.5 asks the narrower question that gap leaves open: can those derivations themselves be formally verified, not just trusted? Ax-Prover's actual tool is Lean, not just a structural analogy anymore, so we test it directly on this project's own oracle. The constants are now formally stated in Lean 4 + Mathlib as corollaries of two foundational derivative theorems — once those proofs are actually discharged (not yet: 6 of 7 still end in sorry, which Lean accepts unconditionally), a wrong differentiation won't be able to hide behind a correct-looking substitution.

theorem curvature_at_saddle (A : ℝ) :
  deriv (V' A 0) 0 = -4 * A := by sorry

7 theorem statements written; 6 still end in sorry, 1 (the b=0 symmetry, pure algebra) is fully proved. Scope, drawn deliberately: Claude Code writes statements — ax-prover writes proofs. Out of scope by design: the Eyring–Kramers rate itself (Fokker–Planck asymptotics, not algebra), any MSM convergence claim, and exact tilted-well positions (irrational roots — brentq is the honest answer there).

Reflection

What this project actually taught me

1 · Built the physics from scratch, not just the formula I needed

  • No prior background in stochastic thermodynamics: worked up Kramers escape theory, the Eyring–Kramers prefactor, detailed balance, and why a rate and an equilibrium ratio are different kinds of "exact."
  • Read Rolland, Bouchet & Simonnet (arXiv:1507.05577) in full.
  • That helped me diagnosing ΔF = 0 in the symmetric well as a structural dead end (curvature-limited switching) not a tuning problem.

2 · Went from reading about scientific tooling to guiding it

  • deeptime's MSM / TICA / Bayesian-UQ machinery: from documentation to deployment. Some previous experience from my PHPB&QOT ICFO project.
  • Designed schema-enforced agent contracts (pydantic-ai) instead of trusting free-text LLM output.
  • Specific lesson: a statistical confidence interval is not an error budget. Systematic bias has to be measured and added in quadrature, not assumed away.

3 · "Tighten the tolerance until something fails" proves nothing

  • Rejected this twice: the convergence-study redesign, and the one-sided-gate question.
  • Manufacturing a rejection against a known-right answer is not the same as finding a real one.
  • Both times the better move was the harder one: test the missing case directly against real data.

4 · An MSM lag time must be verified, not assumed; and no LLM shortcut replaces that

  • Phase 1's fixed lag = 20 was under-converged at high β.
  • Caught only by manually scanning the implied-timescale plateau.
  • AI tooling accelerated everything around this step. It did not replace verifying it by hand.

5 · Handing an optimizer the answer is not a demonstration of search

  • v1's prompt leaked the converged lag to the Optimizer as "a well-motivated starting region."
  • All 8 runs then proposed it verbatim on iteration 1, so the Validator's gate was never tested against a wrong config.
  • The flaw was in the demonstration, not the architecture. Removing the leak let a real search, and a real rejection, happen.
